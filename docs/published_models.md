# Published-model comparability

Primary-paper, supplementary-method and implementation audit, 15 November 2026. This is a post-review addition to the frozen study; it adds no model runs. No published score is ranked against our response or Bliss-residual results.

## Target of the controlled comparison

The benchmark contains 1,475,328 inhibition rows, 365,352 dose units and 22,737 pair-cells from 583 pairs, 38 drugs and 39 cells in DrugComb v1.4. Test pair fold *k*, validation fold *(k+1) mod 5*, remaining three folds train; no refit. Single-agent lookups use training pairs only. External descriptors and untreated-cell expression are independent of combination outcomes. Response metrics use all observation IDs; the interaction metric projects pair-cell Bliss residuals off additive drug effects within each fold/cell panel. Equal-pair-cell and row-weighted versions must be distinguished.

## Existing baseline audit

F0, selected F0 and F2, the original GP and its full-data/F2 extensions, original CF and its dose-aware extension, additive ridge and parameter-free Bliss already exist. The shared runner and tests enforce pair partitions, training-only features, order symmetry and observation alignment. F2, GP-F2 and Bliss receive training-pair single-agent information; descriptor/expression DeepSynergy does not. These input differences must accompany any architectural comparison.

Two DeepSynergy experiments are already validated in [`neural_results.md`](neural_results.md). The original-label PyTorch port uses the original feature vectors and labels, published pair assignments, article architecture/optimizer, a smoothed validation epoch choice, then a four-fold refit. It does not repeat the nested architecture search. Its mean-fold RMSE 16.07 and Pearson 0.726 resemble the publication; its pooled R² 0.503 refers to aggregate Loewe labels. These values do not belong in the inhibition table.

The dose adaptation adds each drug's log dose, centres inhibition on the training mean, uses training-only transforms, the shared validation rule without refit, both-order predictions and all 1,475,328 rows. Its protocol was frozen before test prediction. Fifteen fits cover five folds and three seeds. The validation-only pilot set 48 epochs/patience 8; two fits may be budget-limited. RMSE 18.19 and interaction-residual R² −0.047 therefore describe this adaptation, not the optimum of the published architecture. Existing paired intervals are retained; no experiments are duplicated or retuned after review.

## Additional-experiment decision

DeepSynBa was prioritized by output compatibility. Before any new training, the execution gate required a published runnable configuration, exact output-unit conversion, complete independent inputs for all 38 drugs/39 cells, frozen pair folds and training-only transforms. Budget: at most one model, five folds, seed 2026, one configuration, 90 CPU minutes, no search or GPU campaign. Model/representation replacement and dropping evaluation rows were excluded.

The gate failed. The inspected DeepSynBa package supplies NCI-ALMANAC inputs; no full O'Neil feature set/configuration is present. Its published O'Neil task explicitly excludes missing-feature entities and uses replicate medians. The active code fixes `e_0=100`, adds dose-dependent bias heads and includes a ninth sigma output, whereas the article describes eight surface-parameter heads and a dataset-dependent untreated response. The only supplied configuration uses 976 expression and 768 embedding dimensions, a 2,048-dimensional prediction block and up to 500 epochs. Changing response scale alone does not resolve the missing features or the implementation differences. No conclusion is drawn about whether the extra heads affect accuracy.

comboFM was checked as the secondary response model. Its TensorFlow/TFFM stack and complete O'Neil auxiliary features are not available in the validated environment; its assumed known monotherapies also need reconciliation with our lookup boundary. An identity-only port or substituted expression would be a new adaptation. MatchMaker/JointSyn scalar outputs might support a separately aligned aggregate experiment, but do not specify a dose profile; DeepDDS cannot supply continuous targets through a class-probability transformation.

For a hypothetical score exactly defined as the same weighted mean excess over a known reference, one can add back that reference, subtract Bliss and apply our panel contrast. Exact dose grids, weights, fitted references, units and pair-cell identities are necessary; a label called “Loewe” is insufficient. This transformation still does not identify dose-level response RMSE. Broadcasting a scalar across doses would create a different baseline and confound response accuracy with the imposed profile.

No new predictions were produced. The manuscript now explicitly limits the architectural inference. This audit does not show that published models fail on our interaction target; it shows which direct comparisons remain unevaluated and why published scores cannot fill that gap.
